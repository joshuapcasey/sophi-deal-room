Methodology · Aug 2026

How the model actually works.

SOPHI's 2026 plan isn't a top-down TAM guess or a market-share assumption. It's a per-account revenue build, filtered through four structural gates, priced against SOPHI's own book, and won at the rate SOPHI's history has actually delivered. This page walks the full derivation — the decoupling principle, the three-layer framework, the SOPHI Growth Engine mechanics, and the 50% SAM defensibility ceiling.

Section 01 · The core principle

WAS, SAM, and SOM answer different questions.

The single biggest change from SOPHI's v4 model to v5-onward is a clean separation between three things that had been tangled: sales-readiness scoring, market sizing, and revenue timing. In v4, a single Weighted Account Score (WAS) was driving all three, which meant the SAM number was quietly smuggling in sales judgment about which accounts we could win. LPs would (rightly) push back on that.

Founder direction, locked April 2026

I like the idea of prioritization from a sales strategy but I don't want it to throw off the overarching financial methodology. Review WAS and SAM eliminating redundancies. Then benchmark the SAM total so it is not greater than 50% of TAM.

The fix is architectural. Each layer takes different inputs and answers a different question, and WAS is confined to the sales layer where it belongs.

Layer 1

TAM

"How big is this market in dollars?"
Conservative per-account revenue formulas: rooms × occupancy × valet conversion × rate × days for hotels; seats × turns × conversion × rate × days × peak modifier for restaurants.
Output: $ per market
Layer 2

SAM

"Which dollars are structurally reachable?"
Four objective filters — Partnership, Enterprise operator, Extended-stay, Micro — plus an optional 50% TAM ceiling. Zero WAS input.
Output: $ per market
Layer 3

SOM

"How much, when, in what order?"
WAS drives tier (A/B/C/D) and sign order within SAM. S-curves shape ramp by tier. Capacity ramp caps annual wins at 6 / 14 / 23 / 33 / 43.
Output: $ per year × 5

The result: if a hotel is structurally addressable but operated by someone we have no in with, that's a SOM timing problem, not a SAM problem. The market sizing stays honest. Sales judgment is priced into when revenue arrives, not whether a dollar counts as addressable.

Section 02 · Layer 1 — TAM

Bottom-up, per-account, conservative.

TAM is never a top-down estimate. Every account in the model has a specific address, a real operator, and a modeled annual revenue built from the account's own physical inputs. Hotels use rooms × occupancy × valet conversion × valet rate × 360 days. Restaurants use seats × table turns × valet conversion × rate × operating days × peak modifier. Hospitals, venues, and airports are qualitative pipeline only — $0 modeled TAM but cited as upside.

Hotel formula
TAMhotel = Rooms × Occupancy × ValetConv × ValetRate × 360
Restaurant formula
TAMrest = Seats × Turns × ValetConv × Rate × Days × PeakMod

Key defaults (all uniform across markets)

  • Hotel occupancy: 60% flat (refinable with STR data)
  • Valet conversion (hotels): tiered by hotel type — luxury 75%, upper-upscale 55%, upscale 40%, upper-mid 25%, mid 15%
  • Valet rate: per-market book price, no ADR-based inflation
  • Restaurant turns: 2.5 per operating day (industry benchmark)
  • Peak modifier: 1.1× for weekend-heavy or destination restaurants

These defaults are deliberately below industry-typical. If SOPHI's actual conversion is higher (early anchors run 70-80% at luxury properties), the model still books at the modeled rate. Upside sits outside the number.

Section 03 · Layer 2 — SAM

Four structural filters, one defensibility ceiling.

SAM strips TAM down to the dollars that are actually structurally reachable given how the parking market is bundled. Every filter is objective — no sales judgment enters this layer.

Filter What it removes Why it's out
Partnership Assets operated under PMC bundled contracts or brand partnership agreements Not directly addressable; surfaces via preferred-vendor or PMC channel, not direct sales
Enterprise operator Assets under LAZ, SP+, Ace, Towne Park, Impark multi-property contracts Displacement requires enterprise-level RFP, not single-asset sale. Combined NA share ≈38%.
Extended-stay Home2, Homewood, Hyatt House, SpringHill Suites, Element brands Structurally limited valet upside per asset; product-market fit weak
Micro Accounts with TAM below $150K Below the CAC/LTV floor for direct sales motion

The 50% TAM ceiling

A SAM that runs above 50% of TAM invites LP challenge. We benchmarked this against four sources, and 50% is where vertical B2B tech markets with meaningful self-ops, PMC bundling, and enterprise operators tend to top out.

Source Finding
Wise — TAM SAM SOM guide Vertical B2B SaaS SAM/TAM typically 25-50%, rarely above 60% without strong structural defense
Coherent Market Insights — US Parking Services US parking: 37% self-operated by owner / 63% third-party managed
Marketintelo — Valet Parking Services US valet: 58% outsourced / 42% in-house; NA top-5 operators (SP+, Ace, LAZ, Towne Park, Impark) hold ≈38% combined share
Activant Capital — Vertical Software Research Investors discount SAM >50% of TAM without an explicit structural defense

SOPHI's rolled-up SAM lands at 38.1% of TAM across the SOPHI 6 portfolio — below the defensibility ceiling. Cold markets (Denver, Phoenix, Cleveland) naturally sit at 23-40% because enterprise operator penetration is higher. Charlotte sits closer to 50% because SOPHI already displaced four hotel anchors, which structurally opened the market.

Section 04 · Layer 3 — SOM

Cadence, capacity, and compounding.

SOM is where sales-readiness (WAS) finally enters the model. Every in-SAM account gets a WAS score from SOPHI's proprietary scoring rubric, which drives two decisions: the account's tier (A/B/C/D) and its position in the signing queue. Tier determines the S-curve shape of the ramp; queue position, combined with the capacity ceiling, determines when the account signs.

Capacity ramp (not aspirational)

The plan does not assume unlimited sales throughput. SOPHI models a hard cap on wins per year that grows with headcount:

Year Y1 Y2 Y3 Y4 Y5
Max cumulative wins 6 14 23 33 43
Incremental wins 6 8 9 10 10

These numbers correspond to SOPHI's staffing plan: one full seller in Y1 (six-account book), scaling to four sellers by Y5 (43 accounts). Below the cap, accounts sign in WAS-order within a market. Above the cap, they defer to the next year.

Pattern 1 — Wins compound within an ownership group

The first close inside an ownership group (Marriott portfolio, Hilton portfolio, White Lodging cluster) lifts subsequent same-group accounts' WAS scores by 15-35%. This is a SOPHI-book precedent, not competitor data or web-scraped priors. The multiplier steps are calibrated at 1.5× after one sister-property win, 2× after two, and 3× after three.

Pattern 2 — Cadence, not luck

The close rate holds at roughly one signed account per quarter per active seller. This is the model's core throughput assumption, and it's measured from SOPHI's own history — not asserted. Every seller-quarter in the plan closes at that pace or the year's win count falls short.

Section 05 · Growth Engine mechanics

Three refinements that make the SOM number defensible.

The SOPHI Growth Engine layers three refinements on top of the base bottom-up SOM build. Each is designed to remove a common failure mode of top-down TAM projections and to reflect how SOPHI actually wins accounts. Nothing in the TAM or SAM layers is affected — the refinements sit entirely inside the SOM engine.

Refinement 01
Relationship-multiplier lifecycle

Every account carries a lifecycle status that records how it entered the SOM number: SOPHI anchor, gate-plus-cap win, operator-gated (still in SAM but blocked by operator relationship), cap-deferred (would win but hit the annual ceiling), or below the cap line. The status is visible on every account modal so investors can trace any dollar back to the exact reason it did or didn't book in Y1–Y5.

A binary in-SAM / out-of-SAM flag couldn't distinguish an account we chose to defer from one we couldn't reach. The lifecycle field solves that.

Refinement 02
Hometown advantage (Indianapolis only)

Indianapolis is SOPHI's home market. This layer adds two effects that only apply there: a hometown WAS boost for accounts within a 30-minute drive of SOPHI HQ (reflects on-the-ground presence, faster response, easier pilots), and a Y2 M&A absorption tranche that models the acquisition of a smaller local operator's book. The absorption tranche adds roughly $1.8M to Y2 SOM in Indianapolis and shifts the market from Cold to Warm mid-plan.

Neither effect applies to any market outside Indianapolis. The base engine runs unchanged in Charlotte, Phoenix, Cleveland, Louisville, and Denver.

Refinement 03
Ownership-group ceiling logic

Even when accounts share an ownership group and the relationship multiplier fires, the model now caps same-group wins at 50-60% of the group's in-SAM footprint per market. This prevents the compounding pattern from producing a fantasy outcome where one relationship absorbs an entire portfolio inside a five-year window. Real portfolio penetration takes longer, and the cap enforces that discipline.

Section 07 · Defensibility

What we'd stand behind, and what we wouldn't.

Every number in the SOPHI model can be traced to one of three sources: a per-account measurement from SOPHI's own book, a documented public data point (STR, industry reports linked above), or an explicit modeling assumption disclosed in this document. Nothing is a black-box top-down number.

What we stand behind

  • The TAM formulas. Every input is either a conservative industry default or a per-account measurement. If STR data revises hotel occupancy up, TAM revises up — but not by heroic amounts.
  • The four SAM filters. Each removes revenue for a structural reason (bundling, enterprise operator lock, brand fit, unit economics) that persists regardless of SOPHI's sales execution.
  • The 50% ceiling. Four independent industry sources place the defensibility line here for vertical B2B tech markets with meaningful self-ops.
  • The capacity ramp. 6/14/23/33/43 wins-by-Y5 corresponds to SOPHI's actual staffing plan and pipeline. It is not an aspirational number.
  • The 1× per seller-quarter close rate. Measured from SOPHI's own history — the anchors were closed at approximately this cadence.

What we treat as an open question

  • Group-relationship multipliers. The 1.5×/2×/3× steps are SOPHI-book priors from a small sample. We disclose them explicitly and cap same-group penetration at 50-60% to bound the assumption's impact.
  • Y2 M&A absorption in Indianapolis. The $1.8M tranche assumes a specific acquisition closes on plan. If it doesn't, Indianapolis reverts to the v5 base engine and Y2 SOM drops by that amount.
  • Cold-market close rate. Denver, Phoenix, Cleveland, and Louisville use the same close cadence as Charlotte's warm start. If cold markets ramp slower, the S-curves shift right by one to two quarters, not the win count.

What we do not claim

  • That SOPHI will close 100% of in-SAM accounts by Y5. The plan books 34 wins against 93 in-SAM accounts — a Y5 SAM penetration of 36.6%.
  • That the SAM number is the ceiling. Upside sits outside the model — brand partnerships, PMC channel deals, and hospital/venue verticals are qualitative pipeline, not booked TAM.
  • That the plan is insensitive to sales-team execution. If SOPHI misses one seller-quarter, the model misses one account and roughly $800K at Y5.

The model is a hypothesis about how SOPHI's business grows, framed to be falsifiable at each layer. We'd rather be corrected on one input than have LPs discount the entire number.
