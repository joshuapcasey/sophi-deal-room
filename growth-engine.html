Step 01 · The record

What the record shows

Sophi didn't start from a blank sheet. Before this model existed, the operator team had already signed four Charlotte anchor accounts — real GMs, real revenue, real cadence. That win history is the primary input to every downstream number: it fixes the win rate at ~1 close per quarter, sets the ownership-group tier boost, and calibrates the ramp assumption.

Every ownership tier in the model — the 1.35x / 1.15x / 1.0x WAS multipliers — has a Sophi-book precedent. Nothing here is competitor data or web scrapes; the priors are ours.

Two patterns that drive everything

Pattern 1 — Wins compound within an ownership group. First close inside a group lifts subsequent same-group WAS scores by 15-35%.

Cadence, not luck

Pattern 2 — Close rate holds at roughly one signed account per quarter per active seller. This is the plan's core throughput assumption, and it's measured — not asserted.

Step 03 · Constrain

Constrain — ceiling, pool, and ramp

Not every account is reachable. The pool structure filters TAM through three lenses: ownership ceiling (partnership / PMC / enterprise-operator accounts can't be self-sold and drop out of SAM), economic viability (accounts with TAM under $150K become "micro" and are excluded), and brand exclusion (extended-stay hotels — a known unfit vertical — are out).

Step 04 · Win rate

Win rate from cadence, not conversion theory

Instead of applying a generic conversion-rate to SAM, the model runs a per-account WAS score (Win-Ability Score) which blends: vertical fit, ownership tier boost, geography, operator gate, and cluster proximity to existing wins. Accounts are sorted by WAS and assigned a modeled sign-year 1-5, gated by throughput (one signed account per quarter per seller).

The output — visible in the Accounts table — is the exact list of which accounts sign in Y1, Y2, Y3, Y4, Y5 across the six-market portfolio.

WAS = base × tier_mult × operator_gate × cluster_boost where tier_mult ∈ {1.35 (anchor), 1.15 (adjacent), 1.00 (cold)} and operator_gate ∈ {1.0 (self-park), 0.0 (blocked op)}
Step 05 · Compose

Compose the five-year curve

With sign-year, TAM, and ramp per account, revenue is deterministic. Each account contributes y1..y5 based on months signed and ramp curve; the portfolio SOM is the sum.

Result: Y5 run-rate, cumulative through Y5, with signed accounts inside the six-market SAM.
