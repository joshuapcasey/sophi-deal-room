## Market Overview

Minneapolis–Saint Paul is a dual-core metro (~3.7M) with a skyway-connected downtown Minneapolis and a distinct downtown Saint Paul. The CBD anchors around Nicollet Mall, the Minneapolis Convention Center (480k sq ft; skyway to Hilton and Hyatt Regency), and the Warehouse District / North Loop, which holds the highest concentration of upscale restaurants and lifestyle hotels. US Bank Stadium (66,200 seats) drives event-night parking surges on the CBD's south edge. Satellite demand nodes include Mall of America / Bloomington (two anchor resort hotels), Saint Paul Lowertown (InterContinental, Grand Casino Arena), Edina 50th & France, and Eagan (Omni Viking Lakes).

A critical 2026 signal: Minneapolis is receiving Michelin Guide coverage for the first time, funded by Meet Minneapolis ([MPR News, April 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/08/michelin-guide-expansion-minneapolis-restaurants)). This is expected to elevate fine-dining valet conversion rates in H2 2026, particularly at Demi, Kado no Mise, Spoon and Stable, and Owamni.

## Acquisition Landscape

The local valet tier is fragmented among three small independents. **Piccadilly Valet** is the primary target: sole-owner structure (Andy Holb, active since 1990), confirmed coverage across hotels/restaurants/hospitals/events, and no succession signal. **Class A Valet** and **Unparalleled Parking** are secondary targets with overlapping downtown and suburban coverage. Acquiring any of these would provide immediate operator footprint, staff, and client relationships across the market.

---

## Notable Risks

- **Operator entrenchment**: Towne Park (Marriott/Hyatt) and SP+ (Hilton cluster) hold multi-property agreements limiting single-property entry.
- **Skyway dependency**: Minneapolis's 80-block indoor skyway reduces perceived parking urgency, potentially lowering valet capture vs. street-exposed markets.
- **Seasonality**: Hard winters (Nov–Mar) compress restaurant valet demand ~20%; hotels remain stable from convention traffic.
- **MOA concentration risk**: JW Marriott + Radisson Blu (842 rooms combined) in a single Bloomington node — strong demand but isolated from downtown operating zone.
