# Fort Lauderdale (Broward County) Market Memo
**Market Key:** ft_lauderdale | **Date:** May 2026 | **Accounts:** 103

---

## Market Overview

Fort Lauderdale anchors a 1.9-million-person metro running the length of Broward County. Hospitality density concentrates on **A1A (Fort Lauderdale Beach Boulevard)** — twelve branded hotels in three miles — and in two secondary corridors: the **Port Everglades / 17th Street Causeway** (convention center, Hilton Marina, cruise terminal), and the **Hollywood / Sunrise entertainment belt** (Seminole Hard Rock 1,271-room campus, Diplomat 1,000 rooms, Amerant Bank Arena).

Three demand drivers are structurally distinct from most inland markets: (1) **cruise embarkation** via Port Everglades (~4M passengers/year), generating high-frequency same-day turnover; (2) **beachfront resort density** where valet-only operations are the norm; and (3) **casino-resort infrastructure** where Hard Rock's captive program serves a 24/7 gaming crowd. Seasonality follows a dual peak — winter/spring snowbird season and summer beach/family season — with shoulder periods in May and September.

## Notable Risks

- **Operator concentration:** Towne Park + USA Parking control contracts at 12+ of the top 20 revenue properties. Displacement requires contract expiry or management turnover.
- **Casino lock-in:** Seminole Hard Rock's vertically integrated program (1,271 rooms + Hard Rock Live + Council Oak) is a tribal enterprise — near-zero near-term opportunity.
- **Condo-hotel fragmentation:** Conrad (290 units), Pelican Grand (156 keys), Auberge Beach have fractured ownership structures that complicate procurement.
- **Cruise surge days:** Port Everglades turnaround days create valet bottlenecks throughout the 17th St corridor — a clear insertion opportunity at volume-stressed properties.
