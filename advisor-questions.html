Is a 64% discount to the Organic Growth Valuation the right F&F pricing?

Current placeholder: Round 1 at $4.5M pre against a $12.43M Organic Growth Valuation — a 64% discount. F&F investors get organic-anchored entry with acquisition-case upside optionality, producing 2.16× MOIC at organic-only and 6.31× at Acquisition base case.

Trade-off: Deeper discount rewards F&F for early-mover risk but sets a low anchor that could compress Round 2 pricing. Shallower discount keeps optionality for R2 but may under-reward the F&F pool who's carrying execution risk before Hub ships. Standard F&F discount to next-round pricing is 20–30%, but this is F&F priced against an *earlier* valuation anchor, so the two aren't directly comparable.

Option set:

  • A · 64% discount (current): $4.5M pre, aligns with the deal-room's existing organic-DCF discount math
  • B · 50% discount: $6.2M pre, thinner F&F return but leaves more room for R2 pricing
  • C · Tighter (20% off DCF): $10M pre, treats F&F as a proper priced round with modest discount — F&F becomes a smaller check base

Round 2 pre-money produces a thin 2.6× base-case MOIC. Which lever moves?

Current placeholder: Round 2 at $12M pre / $4.5M raise / 27.3% dilution against a $36.30M Acquisition Growth Valuation. That produces a 2.63× MOIC / 27.4% IRR at base case (Acquisition Growth Valuation), which is thin for institutional seed pricing. Real institutional seed investors typically underwrite for 3–5× at base case.

Trade-off: Lowering the R2 pre-money improves investor returns but increases founder dilution. Raising less money means fewer acquisitions financed. Pushing the story hard on the "Acquisition Growth Model + acquisitions integrated" case ($55M exit, 3.99× R2 MOIC) reframes the base case but assumes acquisitions perform as expected. Fastest path to a defensible R2 story is to reduce raise size, but that means one acquisition instead of two.

Option set:

  • A · Lower R2 pre-money to $8M: R2 investors get 36% at post, 3.9× base MOIC. Founder drops to ~62%.
  • B · Reduce R2 raise to $2.5M: Fund one acquisition instead of two. R2 investors get ~17% at post, still ~2.6× MOIC but at lower absolute risk.
  • C · Keep $12M pre, lean on integrated-case upside: Reframe the R2 investor pitch on the $55M-exit case (3.99× / 41% IRR) not the $36M-exit case. Requires confident acquisition narrative.
  • D · Mix: $10M pre, $4.0M raise. R2 investors 28.6% at post, ~2.9× base MOIC, funds ~$3.5M of acquisitions.

Is a 10% reserve enough for a first-time acquirer buying two operators simultaneously?

Current placeholder: $450K working capital reserve (10% of Round 2). Standard for a repeat acquirer with acquisition muscle. SOPHI has never done an acquisition and is doing two at once.

How do we validate the Human OS 2× properties-per-manager assumption before R2 diligence?

The single largest assumption in the entire SOPHI model is that each Account Manager can carry roughly twice the property count of a traditional operator's manager — the Human OS lever on Moat 02 and the mitigation for Risk #2. The Acquisition Growth Model prices Y5 SOM against this ratio; if measurement lands closer to 1.3–1.5× than 2×, EBIT margin compresses from the 25% target toward 18–20% and the operating-leverage story softens.

What's missing is quantitative controlled data. Waiting for a full year of Charlotte + South Bend measurement before R2 is the most defensible path but doesn't fit the R1→R2 timeline. Publishing an interim confidence band before R2 diligence is the honest middle path, but requires methodology work in the next 60–90 days.

  • C · Resize now to 1.5–1.75×: Rewrite the Acquisition Growth Model on a conservative bandwidth assumption; keep the 2× case as documented upside. Removes the risk entirely from R2 diligence at the cost of a lower Y5 SOM headline.

Q9b · Operating methodology
Priority 2 · Feeds acquisition confidence bands

How often do we recalibrate WAS v2 scoring against closed-deal actuals, and who owns it?

WAS scoring gives every account in the SOPHI 6 a 100-point score across five dimensions of fit, drives tier assignment (A/B/C/D), and orders the signing queue inside the annual capacity caps (6 → 14 → 23 → 33 → 43 accounts across Y1–Y5). It's methodologically well-scaffolded, but the input weights are still calibrated to founder priors rather than to closed-deal actuals. Every closed acquisition is a chance to refine the weights against real signing behavior — or a chance to let the model drift.

Trade-off: Frequent recalibration (after every deal) keeps the model honest but risks over-fitting to the first two data points. Slower recalibration (annual, or after five deals) keeps the model stable but risks propagating a scoring bias into R2's target list. This is also the mitigation for Risk #3 (Acquisition target quality) — without a refresh cadence, WAS-scored TAM and post-close reality drift apart.

Option set:

  • A · Recalibrate after every 2 closed acquisitions: Balances responsiveness with stability. Written into the R2 investor commitments as a methodology discipline. Refresh output goes into an updated market-map + confidence-band publication each cycle.
  • B · Recalibrate annually regardless of deal count: Predictable cadence, easier to communicate, but slower to react if the first two acquisitions surface a scoring bias.
  • C · Recalibrate only when material variance appears: Trigger-based (≥15% actual-vs-scored delta triggers refresh). Lightest process load; hardest to defend as a methodology in R2 diligence.
