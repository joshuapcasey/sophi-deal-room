Priority 1 · Blocks R1

Is a 64% discount to the Organic Growth Valuation the right F&F pricing?

Current placeholder: Round 1 at $4.5M pre against a $12.43M Organic Growth Valuation — a 64% discount. F&F investors get organic-anchored entry with acquisition-case upside optionality, producing 2.16× MOIC at organic-only and 6.31× at Acquisition base case.

Trade-off: Deeper discount rewards F&F for early-mover risk but sets a low anchor that could compress Round 2 pricing. Shallower discount keeps optionality for R2 but may under-reward the F&F pool who's carrying execution risk before Hub ships. Standard F&F discount to next-round pricing is 20–30%, but this is F&F priced against an *earlier* valuation anchor, so the two aren't directly comparable.

Option set:

  • A · 64% discount (current): $4.5M pre, aligns with the deal-room's existing organic-DCF discount math
  • B · 50% discount: $6.2M pre, thinner F&F return but leaves more room for R2 pricing
  • C · Tighter (20% off DCF): $10M pre, treats F&F as a proper priced round with modest discount — F&F becomes a smaller check base

Q2 · Capital structure
Priority 1 · Blocks R2

Round 2 pre-money produces a thin 2.6× base-case MOIC. Which lever moves?

Current placeholder: Round 2 at $12M pre / $4.5M raise / 27.3% dilution against a $36.30M Acquisition Growth Valuation. That produces a 2.63× MOIC / 27.4% IRR at base case (Acquisition Growth Valuation), which is thin for institutional seed pricing. Real institutional seed investors typically underwrite for 3–5× at base case.

Trade-off: Lowering the R2 pre-money improves investor returns but increases founder dilution. Raising less money means fewer acquisitions financed. Pushing the story hard on the "Acquisition Growth Model + acquisitions integrated" case ($55M exit, 3.99× R2 MOIC) reframes the base case but assumes acquisitions perform as expected. Fastest path to a defensible R2 story is to reduce raise size, but that means one acquisition instead of two.

Option set:

  • A · Lower R2 pre-money to $8M: R2 investors get 36% at post, 3.9× base MOIC. Founder drops to ~62%.
  • B · Reduce R2 raise to $2.5M: Fund one acquisition instead of two. R2 investors get ~17% at post, still ~2.6× MOIC but at lower absolute risk.
  • C · Keep $12M pre, lean on integrated-case upside: Reframe the R2 investor pitch on the $55M-exit case (3.99× / 41% IRR) not the $36M-exit case. Requires confident acquisition narrative.
  • D · Mix: $10M pre, $4.0M raise. R2 investors 28.6% at post, ~2.9× base MOIC, funds ~$3.5M of acquisitions.

Q5 · Deal structure
Priority 2

Is a 10% reserve enough for a first-time acquirer buying two operators simultaneously?

Current placeholder: $450K working capital reserve (10% of Round 2). Standard for a repeat acquirer with acquisition muscle. SOPHI has never done an acquisition and is doing two at once.

Trade-off: A larger reserve means less deployed to acquisition price, potentially reducing the size of what SOPHI can buy or forcing an earnout structure. A smaller reserve exposes the company to post-close surprises — unexpected liabilities, key-staff departures, contract non-renewals, integration cost overruns.

Option set:

  • A · Keep 10%: Standard, keeps acquisition budget higher
  • B · 15–20% reserve: First-time-acquirer prudent
  • C · Earnout structure on both deals: Reduces upfront cash need, keeps reserve tight, aligns operator incentives — but requires operator agreement

Should the ESOP pool expand between Round 1 and Round 2?

Current placeholder: 100,000 shares (10% of pre-round cap) allocated to ESOP, unchanged across both rounds. That's 7.83% of post-R2 cap. Institutional Series A rounds typically require a 10–15% option pool refresh — Round 2 investors may push for the same.

Trade-off: Refreshing the pool before Round 2 is founder-friendly on paper — the dilution comes from pre-money — but Round 2 investors may still push. Not refreshing may make Round 2 harder to close on standard terms. Adding pool at Round 1 dilutes F&F too, which reduces their return.

Option set:

  • A · Keep 10% pool, no refresh: Cleanest founder position but may create R2 friction
  • B · Refresh pool to 15% pre-R2: Standard institutional expectation, dilutes founder from 70.4% → 65.6%
  • C · Refresh pool to 12.5% pre-R2: Compromise, dilutes founder to 68% at post-R2

Q7 · Terms
Priority 2

Round 1 as priced round or SAFE — which better serves the F&F pool and Round 2 close?

Current placeholder: Priced round OR post-money SAFE @ $5M cap. Priced is cleaner cap-table-wise and lets F&F investors see their exact ownership. SAFE is faster/cheaper to close but adds complexity at R2 conversion.

Trade-off: Priced round requires more legal work up-front ($15–30K per YC/Cooley template) but gives F&F investors a real share count and pre-emptive rights. SAFE gets money in the door in a week, but F&F investors sometimes push back on SAFE terms (no pre-emptive rights, no board seat, ambiguous conversion mechanics). SAFE conversion at Round 2 also gets messy if pre-money moves.

How much time between Round 1 close and Round 2 close?

Current placeholder: ~12 months (Jan 2027 → early 2028). Gives SOPHI enough time to ship Hub MVP, demonstrate Y1 Acquisition SOM traction, and finalize acquisition LOIs. But 12 months is short for institutional Round 2 diligence and closing if diligence prep isn't already in place.

Board composition after Round 2 — how many R2 seats?

Currently unaddressed in the deal room. Standard institutional Round 2 with 13% ownership would expect 1 board seat. SOPHI's board currently has zero outside members (founder + potentially F&F observer).

Trade-off: A defined governance structure (3 founder + 1 R2 + 1 independent) is the institutional-friendly path and gives Round 2 real skin in the game.
